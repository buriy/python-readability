People who can't reply to posts as part of the new test will still be able to see and share them

Twitter has begun testing a new feature which allows its users to limit who can reply to their posts.

The social network announced on Wednesday that a small group of users will be able to block replies to their posts or only allow certain people to respond.

"Sometimes, unwanted replies make it hard to have meaningful conversations," the company's director of product management Suzanne Xie wrote in a blog post.

People with access to the new feature are given an option while writing a new post to choose who can reply to it once it's published. They can select everyone on Twitter, only people they follow, or only people who are mentioned in the tweet.

Others users who are unable to reply to the tweet can still see it in their timeline and retweet or like the post.

Twitter first announced the new feature in January and said it would be launched at some point this year.

The social network has made a series of updates designed to improve conversations on the app and reduce the ability of trolls to share abuse.

Last year, it began allowing users to hide replies to their tweets, a potential way to limit the spread of abuse online.
